# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

Welcome to the OctoAcme Project Management documentation hub. This README is the entry point for new team members and stakeholders to navigate all standardized process documents and understand the key workflows used across cross-functional projects.

## Overview

OctoAcme follows a structured project management lifecycle that guides work from initial idea through to production release and continuous improvement. Every project begins with an **Initiation** phase — validating the problem statement, identifying stakeholders, and establishing high-level goals. This flows into **Planning**, where scope, milestones, resources, and dependencies are defined in detail. During **Execution & Tracking**, the delivery team builds and iterates in short cycles, managing blockers and reporting progress through a regular cadence of standups and weekly syncs between the Project Manager (PM) and Product Manager (PdM). Work is considered done only when acceptance criteria are met and the Definition of Done is satisfied.

OctoAcme projects involve a set of clearly defined **roles and personas**: the Project Manager coordinates delivery, schedules, and communications; the Product Manager owns the roadmap and success metrics; Developers design, implement, and test features; QA validates quality and acceptance criteria; and Stakeholders provide input and approvals. Each project is anchored by a named PM and Product Lead to ensure clear ownership and accountability throughout the lifecycle.

**Risk management and communication** are treated as ongoing activities, not one-time events. The team maintains a risk register throughout execution, assessing impact and likelihood, assigning owners, and reviewing status at weekly syncs. Stakeholder communication follows a regular cadence — weekly status updates, monthly stakeholder briefings, and ad-hoc escalations along a defined path (Team → PM → Product Lead → Sponsor). For incidents, a structured communication template and blameless post-incident retrospective ensure transparency and learning.

**Quality assurance and release practices** are built into every stage. Pre-release requirements include passing CI pipelines, security scans, merged PRs with all acceptance criteria met, drafted release notes, and documented rollback plans. Deployments follow a standardized checklist covering staging smoke tests, production deployment via automated pipeline, post-deploy verification, and stakeholder announcements. After each release, a **Retrospective** captures learnings, drives continuous improvement, and feeds back into future planning cycles.
